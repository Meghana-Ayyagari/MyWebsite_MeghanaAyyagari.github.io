Journey of Physics

Where It All Began?

It all started with my grandfather’s library of Physics books — from those little 15-page books that sparked curiosity to proper problem-solving textbooks and a few rare reference books that might be a little harder to find today. I often used to watch him make Demo-Models on various concepts such as Oscillations,Solar System,Simple Pendulum, etc, which ignited the "Why's" and "How's" in me.

But what truly draws me closer to Physics is his collection of personal notes. They inspire me to explore the subject not just as an amusing world of ideas and phenomena, but as a discipline that teaches patience, consistency, and presence of mind.

A Bit About My Grandfather

He was one of the best Physics lecturers of his time, well known in his hometown and across the universities and colleges where he taught. He also had the opportunity to set a few EAMCET papers over the years.

Where I am now?

Although he planted the seed of interest, choosing Physics was my own decision, driven by my curiosity to explore both its beautiful ideas and its challenging bits.

Well, for me, it’s almost always the Math that feels difficult at first. But once I finally crack it, I find myself thinking, “AH! That was it?”

Pursuing Physics at the Master’s level has added another dimension to this journey. Learning from faculty who dedicate their lectures to nurturing curiosity, critical thinking, and presence of mind has made me appreciate the subject even more. Somewhere along the way, I’ve come to enjoy not just understanding Physics, but also the process of struggling with an idea until it finally makes sense.

Projects

Monte Carlo Simulation of an Ideal Gas

Master's mini-project · Statistical Mechanics & Numerical Methods in Physics

Abstract. In this project, we built a Monte Carlo simulation of an ideal gas to see how random, microscopic particle movements lead to large-scale thermodynamic behaviors. Our simulation successfully matched theoretical expectations, showing the natural emergence of Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distributions and the ideal gas law. We also found that using periodic boundary conditions was absolutely necessary to get accurate results and avoid edge effects in our simulation box.
